I've been building Smartspeed, a research build of sunnypilot for the comma 4: you press cruise and the car picks its own speed. It runs on my own comma 4 in a Peugeot 3008, it still has a lot to learn, and I'm looking for people to help me confirm speed signs. Everything about it, and the sign-up, is on the Smartspeed page.

Why

The map alone isn't enough

sunnypilot's Speed Limit Assist takes the limit from the car or from the map. Many cars send none, so it often comes down to the map, and a map misses a lot: temporary and variable limits, limits nobody has mapped, roadworks. Without a good GPS fix it doesn't even know which road you're on. That's why I started reading the signs with the road camera.

A camera alone wasn't enough either. Signs get hidden or are meant for the exit lane, and on some roads no sign tells you the speed at all. These three are the ones that bother me most, and Smartspeed doesn't handle them yet:

A limit with no end sign

Motorway roadworks often have none, and one missed end sign once held me at 80 for a whole stretch of motorway.

After a motorway ramp

The limit posted on the ramp stops counting on the acceleration lane, and nothing tells you so.

Car parks

The limit of the road outside doesn't apply in there, and usually no sign gives you another one.

A rule for each of these would never end, so I decided to let a network with memory learn them from how I drive.

On the comma

Four small networks, 21 MB in total

They run on the comma 4's GPU next to openpilot's driving model, which still does the driving. Three of them read the signs, and the fourth, the one I call the brain, decides the speed.

What runs on the comma. The brain never reads the map: the map only comes back through sunnypilot's resolver if the brain can't load.

SignNet only looks at a band across the top of the picture, where signs stand, because the median sign is 17 pixels wide and the rest of the picture only costs time. The classifier reads each box it finds. The applicability network is the newest one: it looks at a sign and its surroundings and says whether that sign is for our road or for the one next to it, so a 40 on an exit doesn't slow me down on the motorway. One piece is still a hand-written rule: a number counts once two pictures within 1.5 seconds agree.

The brain

A small network with memory picks the speed

The brain on the car since 12 November, 2026-11-11_2241-brain-s0, trained on one of my two V100s.

Gas, brake and the car's own speed are not inputs on purpose. The answer it learns is my own speed, and a brain I trained with them on 10 November simply followed whatever speed I was already doing on the road. The sign's number also reaches the output directly, through a weight the memory sets, because as a plain input the network learned the road and ignored the sign. The plate under a sign (hours, wet road) is wired in, but no drive in training had one yet, so this brain leaves it out.

How it learns

It learns from how I drive

I drive with sunnypilot switched off in sunnylink, so the comma doesn't drive: it records the video and the logs, and every speed in them is mine. A drive where openpilot was on is left out whole. The answer is the speed my dashboard showed. Where I drove wrong, I draw a block with the right speed in the portal, and that's the only correction. During training, on half of the sign passages where I drove at the sign's number, number and answer get swapped (50 and 60, 100 and 120), otherwise it learns the road and ignores the sign.

Publish exports all four networks to ONNX and runs them against PyTorch, the brain along a whole drive one step at a time as on the car, and only then rebuilds the branch the comma updates from.

The portal

I run all of it from my own server

Video and logs play in sync, with what the camera read, the map, sunnypilot's resolver and the car's state on one timeline. Labelling signs, training, replaying a drive with new networks and publishing all start from there. More than 15,000 frames of signs are confirmed so far, most of them by me and the rest by an AI review, and SignNet and the classifier also pretrain on Mapillary.

Where it stands

11.8 hof dashcam driving in 34 drives, the only ones it learns from

These numbers are offline, for the brain the comma downloaded on 12 November. On validation it's 22.7 % and 10.3 km/h on 3,586 seconds. On the training drives it gets 94.6 %, so it still knows the roads it has seen far better than new ones. Where a sign is in force, simply copying it is still right more often on test (43.9 % against 27.2 %), with the same mean error of 10.0 km/h. When I swap the number on the test signs, its answer moves on only 120 of 2,208 seconds, so it still leans on the road more than on the sign. Honestly, this training didn't beat the brain before it: the sign reading got better, the brain stayed where it was.

On the road there's very little so far. A brain has been on the car since 7 November, but since then I've driven with sunnypilot off to collect training drives, so the car hasn't acted on what it says yet. This one has no drive yet.
